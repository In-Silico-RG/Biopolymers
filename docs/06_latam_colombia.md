# 06 — Phase 3: Latin America and Colombia, as an argument

*2026-09-24. Phase 3 of `00_plan.md`. The numbers exist in the tables of documents 01, 02
and 05; this document says what they mean.*

**Objective.** State, and defend, what the map shows about the position of Latin America and
of Colombia in the biopolymers field and in the computational layer sitting on top of it.

**Status.** closed 2026-09-24. Written on the combined English, Spanish and Portuguese
corpus, screened as well as counted, with the English-only figures kept beside it wherever
the two differ.

---

## 1. The claim

**Latin America is not behind in computational practice. It is small, invisible, and
unorganised, and those are three different problems with three different remedies.** The
region's difficulty is not that it lacks access to molecular dynamics or machine learning.
It is that its output is thin in absolute terms, that a large part of what exists is not
seen by the instruments that count science, and that the part which does use computational
methods has neither a community nor a venue.

Colombia is a sharp case of all three, and an unusually well-connected one.

## 2. Size: small, growing fast, and fourth in the region

| Context | Works, all years | 2015–2025 | Share of world | Growth per year | Open access |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| World | 150,122 | 93,825 | 100% | 11.1% | 43.9% |
| Ibero-America | 14,411 | 10,394 | 11.1% | 12.3% | 61.3% |
| Latin America | 9,416 | 6,944 | 7.4% | 12.5% | 60.0% |
| Colombia | 755 | 566 | 0.60% | 18.9% | 72.7% |

Combined vocabulary, `outputs/tables/T34_combined_context_comparison.csv`.

Latin America holds 7.4% of the world's biopolymer literature and grows faster than the
world does. Inside the region the order is Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Colombia, Chile;
Brazil alone is roughly half of it. Colombia's 18.9% annual growth is the fastest of the
four contexts, on a base small enough that the rate describes the base more than the
capacity.

Three quarters of Colombian output is open access, against 44% worldwide. The region
publishes openly. That is a structural advantage it is not currently getting credit for.

## 3. Invisibility: three separate causes, all measured

A count of Latin American research in this field understates it, and the understatement has
three independent sources. Each was measured, none is assumed.

**Language.** Spanish and Portuguese search terms add 15.4% more works for Latin America and
26.3% for Colombia on the core field, rising to 31.2% for Colombian cellulose and 34.1% for
Colombian polyhydroxyalkanoates. A quarter of the Colombian corpus is in Spanish: 185 works
against 545 in English.

**Affiliation matching.** 12.7% of authorships in the world method corpus match no
institution entity at all, so the country they belong to receives no credit. The loss is
worst for the smallest producers: 27% for Bolivia, 10% for Venezuela, 7% for Uruguay. A 2025
paper carrying two Peruvian affiliations and one Mexican one counts, in the data, as Mexican
alone. Because every regional corpus here was selected on institution country, works whose
regional affiliations all went unmatched never entered it.

**Choice of source.** Lens holds 91% of the OpenAlex world corpus but 66% of the Latin
American one and 57% of the Colombian one. The divergence between two indexes is widest
exactly where this study focuses, so any single-source regional figure is provisional.

None of these is a statement about the quality of the research. They are statements about
the infrastructure that counts it.

## 4. The finding that changes the argument

Rebuilding the corpus in three languages was expected to raise the regional computational
share. It did the opposite, and the reason is the most useful result in this document.

| | Works added by Spanish and Portuguese |
|---|---|
| Core corpora, all contexts | 7,106 |
| Method corpora, all contexts | 19 |

Colombia gains 26.3% of core output from the combined vocabulary and **0.0%** of method
output. Latin America gains 15.4% and 1.4%. The literature that English-language queries
miss is almost entirely non-computational.

### Confirmed by reading, not only by keyword

The keyword figures above could be an artefact of the English-language method vocabulary
failing on Spanish-language abstracts. The screening was therefore re-run on the combined
corpus, and it confirms the finding while correcting its strength.

| Corpus | Materials works added | Of which computational |
|---|---|---|
| Colombia, whole field | 127 | 2 |
| Latin America, whole field | 1,114 | 17 |

The recovered literature is **1.5% computational**, against 4.0% in the English-only corpora
it joins: about four tenths as computational, not zero. The keyword match said 0.0% for
Colombia because the English method terms do not fire on Spanish abstracts; reading finds a
little, and a little is the right answer.

The screened shares therefore fall too, and these are the figures to quote:

| Context | Screened share, English-only corpus | Screened share, combined corpus |
|---|---|---|
| Colombia, whole field | 4.48% | **3.84%** |
| Latin America, whole field | 3.43% | **3.12%** |

Two readings are available and they are not exclusive. The regional computational work is
largely internationalised already: researchers doing molecular dynamics or machine learning
on biopolymers publish in English and were mostly not invisible. And the Spanish- and
Portuguese-language regional literature is predominantly experimental and applied.

The consequence is arithmetic and it cuts against the region. With a complete corpus every
measure of regional computational intensity falls, because the denominator grew much faster
than the numerator: the keyword share for Colombia from 1.94% to 1.59%, the screened share
from 4.48% to 3.84%, and the growth rate from 27.0% to 18.9% as older Spanish-language work
fills in the early years.

**The language correction therefore does not excuse the thin computational layer. It sharpens
it.** Measured on a corpus that finally includes the region's own languages, Latin America's
computational intensity is below the world's rather than level with it.

## 5. What the region actually works on

| Line | World | Latin America | Colombia | Latin America's share of world |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cellulose and nanocellulose | 392,424 | 18,641 | 1,183 | 4.8% |
| Lignin | 186,341 | 13,225 | 956 | 7.1% |
| PHA / PHB | 23,213 | 1,643 | 118 | 7.1% |

The region holds a larger share of world output in lignin and polyhydroxyalkanoates than in
cellulose, which fits a research base working from agro-industrial residues rather than from
purified feedstocks. That is a comparative position, not a deficiency.

On the screened, materials-only subset, Colombia concentrates on chitosan at 22% of its
computational work against a world 9%, and on lignin at 11% against 4.7%. Its method mix
also differs: quantum chemistry leads at 33% where the world leads with molecular dynamics
at 34%. An infrastructural reading is available, since sustained molecular dynamics needs
computing time that single-molecule quantum chemistry does not, but the Colombian
denominator is nine works and this belongs in a discussion as a hypothesis, not in results
as a finding.

## 6. Colombia is the most connected corpus measured

| Corpus | International collaboration | Two or more Latin American countries |
|---|---|---|
| World, method layer | 30.4% | 0.6% |
| Latin America, whole field | 34.4% | 6.0% |
| Colombia, whole field | 44.1% | 23.9% |

Nearly a quarter of Colombian biopolymer works involve a second Latin American country, four
times the regional average. Its strongest partners are Mexico (49 works), Brazil (46) and
Spain (46), a balanced profile.

Brazil is the contrast. Its strongest ties run outward, to the United States (228 works),
Portugal (210) and Spain (141), and dwarf its ties to Argentina (51) or Colombia (46). The
largest producer in the region is the least regionally integrated one.

This matters for what a recommendation can say. Colombia does not need to be taught to
collaborate. It already collaborates more, and more regionally, than the countries above it.

## 7. The structural diagnosis

Two measurements say the same thing from different directions.

**There is no standing computational community.** Of the 14,861 authors in the world method
layer, 86.3% appear exactly once. The fitted Lotka exponent is 3.46 against a classic 2, and
the method layers are the steepest of every corpus measured. This is the profile of a field
before a speciality forms, not after one has matured.

**And it has no home venue.** Bradford's core zone, the journals carrying the first third of
a corpus, is 2.4% of journals worldwide and 2.2% for the Latin American field as a whole.
For the Latin American method layer it takes 13.2% of its journals to reach the first third.
That work is scattered with no venue acting as a centre.

Inside Colombia the institutional base is real but shallow: Universidad Nacional de Colombia
(96 works), Universidad del Valle (66), Universidad de Cartagena (47), Universidad de
Antioquia (47), with Universidad de Sucre at 25.

## 8. What follows

The three problems have three different remedies, and conflating them is how this kind of
study usually goes wrong.

1. **Scale is a funding and continuity problem**, not a training one. The region publishes
   competently and openly; it publishes little, and the people who do computational work do
   it once.
2. **Invisibility is an infrastructure problem with identified causes.** Language,
   affiliation matching and source choice are each measurable and each fixable, two of them
   by the databases rather than by the researchers. Depositing with complete, matchable
   affiliations is the part authors control.
3. **The absence of a community and a venue is an organisational problem**, and it is the
   one a research group can actually move. A recurring regional venue for computational work
   on biopolymers would address a gap this study measured rather than assumed.

The clearest opening remains the generative frontier. Reading every generative work in the
field leaves four credible biopolymer-specific studies worldwide and none from Latin America
(`04_generative_frontier.md`). One of them, on population-aware generation of lignin
ensembles, addresses the same problem as this group's existing lignin line.
